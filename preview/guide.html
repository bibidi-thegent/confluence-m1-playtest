What this is

The same combat sandbox as the M1 playtest, with every class in it: all eight Expertises and both Disciplines of each, 16 in all. A Discipline is a class's role and style: Bulwark can be Bastion, a tank, or Warbreaker, a heavy hitter.

  • It's unfinished. Bastion and Tempest are the two M1 classes. The other 14 are new and most of them are still being built, so you'll find gaps and bugs.
  • The numbers aren't final. Damage, healing and threat haven't been measured yet, so a class that seems far too strong or too weak may just need tuning. Tell us anyway.
  • It's not the M1 playtest. There's no script and no time limit. Play whatever you like, for as long as you like. Your preview files are kept apart from the playtest's results.

Picking a Discipline

  1. Type your tester name in the Tester box on the start screen. It goes into your file names.
  2. Pick a Discipline from the list. They're grouped into Tanks, Healers and DPS; a gate badge marks the two M1 classes and a preview badge the new ones.
  3. To change later, press K out of combat. The menu at the top of Loadout and Talents lists every Discipline. Choose one, then click Equip. Y swaps back to the one you had before.
Settings (O) has Show unfinished Disciplines, on in the preview. Turn it off and the lists keep only the Disciplines that have passed their final checks.

Trying healers and tanks

Healers need someone to heal and tanks need something to hold. Press B for the Debug panel and find Practice fixtures. Each one sets up on the training ground's east side; Stop ends it.

  • Ally Drill: four Practice Allies (one Tank Ally and three Party Allies) stand by the Pain Totem, which hurts them, and you, on a pattern: Steady, Pulse or Flood, with the allies stacked, spread or moving. Keep them alive. Left click an ally or its party frame to focus it, or press F1–F4. A heal goes to the ally under your cursor, then your focus, then you.
  • Tank Drill: the Sparring Champion, a big enemy that can't be moved or stunned. Its Pressure hits every two seconds and its Crush drops a circle to step out of, block or negate. Choose whether it hits whoever it targets or always you, and whether the Practice Allies join in. Take its attention and keep it.
  • Threat Drill: a pack of three or five Sparring Dummies, waiting for your pull or pulled at once, with Striker allies dealing damage beside you. Every so often a Dummy breaks off to attack someone else. Win it back with taunts and threat, and keep the pack on you.

What to tell us

  • The resource: does each Discipline's resource read on the bar under your health? Can you tell when it's full, low or locked without reading the numbers?
  • The Talent button (Q): does it light when you'd expect it to, and can you tell why it lit or went dark?
  • The new ground shapes: traps, walls, Fissures, Songs and heals. Do they read clearly? Does any of them ever look like an enemy attack? Your own heals are green.
  • Healing: do your heals land on the ally you meant?
  • Tanking: do your taunts hold? Does an enemy that slips away come back when you expect?
  • Anything broken: a button that does nothing, a number that makes no sense, something stuck on screen. If you can, press F12 and send a screenshot of the Console's red errors.
  • And which Disciplines you'd want to play again, and why.

Known rough edges

  • The numbers are unmeasured: expect some Disciplines to hit much harder or softer than others.
  • Traps, walls and tethers can stay on the ground after you swap Discipline.
  • The preview shares this browser's settings, keybinds and Bastion and Tempest loadouts with the main page, because they're on the same site.
  • Browser windows inside other apps block mouse capture, as in the main playtest. Open the preview in Chrome or Edge itself.

Sending files and feedback

Saving works the same way as in the main playtest. Press P for the Playtest panel and click Choose telemetry folder once after you start; the game then saves your file every 30 seconds and after each fight. If your browser can't pick a folder, click Download JSON at the end.

Preview files are named m1-preview-…, so they never mix with the playtest's. When you're done, send Bibidi every m1-preview- .json file in your folder, the way Bibidi asked, with your notes. The notes box at the bottom of the Playtest panel's survey also goes into your file.

Privacy

Your files hold gameplay data (what you pressed, where attacks landed, your fights, the Disciplines you played and your notes) and the tester name you type. They also note your browser version, screen size and graphics card for the frame-rate check. Nothing else: no account, email or location.
